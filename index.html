About

I am a PhD candidate in Mechanical Engineering at Carnegie Mellon University, advised by Dr. Noelia Grande Gutiérrez in the Biomedical Flows Simulation and Multiscale Modeling (BioSiMM) Lab. My research develops physics-based computational models of blood flow and thrombosis in arteries.

My dissertation focuses on particle-level modeling of arterial thrombosis initiation. I am developing a hybrid continuum–discrete framework in which individual platelets are transported through arterial-scale flows and undergo adhesion, activation, and aggregation at the vessel wall. Related work has examined the mechanisms of near-wall platelet enrichment in arterial flows and the sensitivity of coronary hemodynamics and diagnostic indices to vascular anatomy and microvascular resistance.

This work draws on computational fluid dynamics, particle-based and multiscale numerical methods, and parallel high-performance computing. It is supported by a scholarship from the Natural Sciences and Engineering Research Council of Canada (NSERC) and was previously supported by a Digital Health Fellowship from the CMU School of Computer Science.

I received an MS in Mechanical Engineering from Carnegie Mellon in 2025 and a BASc in Mechanical Engineering from the University of Waterloo in 2022. At Waterloo, I conducted undergraduate research in the Materials in Biological Systems Lab and completed co-op terms in mechanical design, manufacturing, and computational modeling.
